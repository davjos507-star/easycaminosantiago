History & Tradition

The Camino de Santiago:
A thousand years of faith, culture and human adventure

"The Camino de Santiago is the only European pilgrimage route declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site, in 1985. Each year, more than 300,000 people from all over the world walk its paths."

Who was the Apostle James?

James the Greater was one of Jesus' first disciples and the first apostle to be martyred: King Herod had him beheaded around 44 AD. According to tradition, his disciples carried his body to the shores of Galicia.

In the 9th century, the discovery of his tomb gave rise to centuries of pilgrimage. King Alfonso II ordered a church to be built on the site — the seed of today's Cathedral of Santiago.

The birth of pilgrimage

The news spread across Europe and pilgrimage to Compostela soon rivalled Rome and Jerusalem. The 11th and 12th centuries were its golden age: kings and religious orders built roads, bridges and hospices.

The Camino survived the Black Death and centuries of decline. Its great revival came in the 20th century, and today more than 300,000 people a year walk its paths from more than 180 countries.

The Holy Year

Celebrated when 25 July — the Feast of the Apostle — falls on a Sunday. In a Holy Year, the Holy Door of the Cathedral opens and pilgrims may receive plenary indulgence.

The Compostela

The official certificate issued by the Cathedral, with the pilgrim's name inscribed in Latin. Awarded for walking at least 100 km or cycling 200 km. Today over 300,000 are issued each year.

The Pilgrim's Blessing

A medieval tradition celebrated in various churches along the route before departure. A rite that unites faith, history and the pilgrim community across the centuries.

The route
The Portuguese charm all the way to Compostela

The Portuguese Camino is the world's fastest-growing pilgrimage route. Approx. 240 km from Porto Cathedral through the green interior of Portugal, crossing the river Miño at Tui and into Galicia all the way to the Plaza del Obradoiro. Barcelos, Ponte de Lima and Pontevedra are just some of the places that will define the journey.

Ideal for those looking to experience authentic Portugal on foot and discover two cultures in a single trip. Our agency in Lugo knows every accommodation and every stretch of the route, offering an experience organised for your complete peace of mind.

FAQ
Frequently asked questions
What fitness level do I need for the Portuguese Camino?
Moderate fitness required. Stages range from 24 to 33 km with some elevation gain. We recommend prior hiking experience with walks of 12–15 km. Suitable for pilgrims with good physical condition and a background in walking.
Do I need a visa or passport to cross from Portugal to Spain?
No visa is required. Portugal and Spain are both members of the Schengen Area and the European Union. Your standard ID card or passport is all you need to cross the border at the Valença–Tui bridge — no formalities.
Is luggage transfer included?
Yes, in all packages. Your main rucksack travels each morning to the next accommodation while you walk with a small daypack. We manage the service across both countries.
When is the best time to walk the Portuguese Camino?
Year-round, though spring (March–June) and autumn (September–November) are the most pleasant seasons. Summers are drier but warmer. Winter offers spectacular scenery with fewer pilgrims on the path.
How far in advance should I book?
We recommend 4–6 weeks for mid-season, and 2–3 months for Easter and bank holiday weekends. In low season we can often organise everything in under a week.
