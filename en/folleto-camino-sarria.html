History & tradition

The Camino de Santiago:
A thousand years of faith, culture and human adventure

"The Camino de Santiago is the only European route declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site, in 1985. Each year, more than 300,000 people from around the world walk its paths."

Who was the Apostle James?

James the Great, son of Zebedee, was one of the first four disciples chosen by Jesus, alongside Peter, Andrew and John. A fisherman on the Sea of Galilee, Jesus nicknamed him and his brother "Sons of Thunder" for their passionate, driven nature.

He was the first apostle to be martyred: King Herod Agrippa I ordered his beheading around 44 AD. According to tradition, his disciples transported his body by ship to the coast of Galicia, where he was buried in what is today Compostela.

In the 9th century, the hermit Pelayo observed mysterious lights over a field. Upon investigation, a tomb was discovered which Bishop Teodomiro identified as that of the Apostle. King Alfonso II ordered a church built over the sepulchre — the origin of today's Cathedral.

The birth of the pilgrimage

News of the discovery spread throughout Europe and pilgrims soon began arriving from every corner of Christendom. By the 10th century the pilgrimage to Compostela was well established, rivalling journeys to Rome and Jerusalem.

The 11th and 12th centuries were its greatest era. Kings such as Sancho III and Alfonso VI drove the route forward, building roads, bridges and hospices. The Cluniac orders organised the infrastructure that would make the Camino Francés world-famous.

The Camino survived periods of decline — the Black Death, the Wars of Religion — but always revived. In 1884, Pope Leo XIII reaffirmed the authenticity of the relics, and the 20th century brought the greatest revival in modern history.

The Holy Year

Celebrated when 25th July — the feast of the Apostle — falls on a Sunday. Pope Calixtus II established this privilege in 1122. During a Holy Year, the Holy Door of the Cathedral opens and pilgrims can receive plenary indulgence.

The Compostela

The official certificate issued by the Cathedral. The pilgrim's name is inscribed in Latin and signed by the Cathedral Secretary. In 1985 just 2,491 were awarded; today over 300,000 are issued each year. It requires walking at least 100km or cycling 200km.

The Pilgrim's Blessing

A medieval tradition held in Roncesvalles at the start of the Camino Francés and in other churches along the route. The cleric prays for the pilgrim to "set out on the journey and return safe and sound with God's help." A rite uniting faith, history and community.

Arzúa → O Pedrouzo

The penultimate stage. You walk through dense eucalyptus forests that create an almost magical atmosphere. In O Pedrouzo, anticipation runs high — tomorrow you reach Santiago. The excitement is palpable in every conversation.

Stage 5 — The arrival
O Pedrouzo → Santiago de Compostela
19.4 km +250m / -280m 5–6 hours
The most special day. You cross Monte do Gozo — the "Hill of Joy" — from where you see the Cathedral towers for the first time. You enter Santiago through the old town. The Plaza del Obradoiro awaits with its embrace of the Apostle and the Pilgrim Mass. A moment you will never forget.

FAQ
Frequently asked questions
Can I get the Compostela starting from Sarria?
Yes. The Cathedral requires a minimum of 100km on foot. Our package covers 115km, exceeding that minimum. You simply need to get your credential stamped at least twice per stage.
What fitness level do I need?
Easy to moderate. Stages of 19–28km with gentle elevation changes. We recommend having walked 10–15km on a previous occasion. People aged 10 to 75 have completed it successfully.
Is bag transfer included?
Yes, in all packages. Your main bag travels each morning to the next accommodation while you walk with a small daypack.
When is the best time to go?
April to June and September to October. The Camino can be done year-round from Sarria — there are no mountain passes at risk of snow. We always recommend bringing a waterproof jacket.
How far in advance should I book?
We recommend 4–6 weeks in high season, 2–3 months for Easter and public holidays. In low season we can often organise everything in less than a week.
